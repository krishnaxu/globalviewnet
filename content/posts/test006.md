  常用的声成像方法之一叫波束形成。可以把它理解为计算机轮流把注意力转向不同位置：先假设声音来自某一点，按这一假设补偿各路信号的传播延时，再把信号叠加。如果假设接近真实声源，同一声喇叭的波形就更容易对齐，叠加后的响应更强；扫描许多候选位置，就得到一张声源分布图。


  这就是常说的声学相机的基本思路。它接收车辆已经发出的声音，并不需要主动向汽车发射声波，道路上常叫做“声呐抓拍”。

  The same sound from a horn propagates to different microphones, with the paths slightly differing, resulting in varying arrival times. Using a simplified calculation based on a sound speed of approximately 340 meters per second, a path difference of 0.34 meters would result in a time difference of about 1 millisecond. Humans find it difficult to directly perceive such a time interval, but electronic systems can detect it by analyzing synchronized waveform recordings.

  However, the time difference detected by a pair of microphones can only narrow down the possible area where the sound source might be located, rather than directly determining its exact position. More microphones provide additional constraints, and when combined with information such as array geometry and road coverage area, it becomes possible to gradually narrow down the potential location of the sound source.


  One of the commonly used sound imaging methods is called beamforming. It can be understood as the computer sequentially directing its attention to different positions: first assuming the sound originates from a specific point, then compensating for the propagation delays of each signal according to this assumption, and finally overlaying the signals. If the assumption is close to the actual sound source, the waveform of the same sound source will align more easily, resulting in a stronger combined response. By scanning many candidate positions, a sound source distribution map is obtained.


  This is the basic concept of the commonly referred to acoustic camera. It receives the sounds already emitted by vehicles and does not need to actively emit sound waves toward the car, which is commonly called "sonar capture" on the road.
